* Engine-agnostic kinetics

Saddle-point searches in =rgsaddle= and local or Riemannian minimizers in =rgmin= query static energy and gradient surfaces @@rst::cite:`goswamiEfficientExploration2025,goswamiEnhancedClimbingImage2026,goswamiGaussianProcessSaddle2025,goswamiAdaptivePruning2026,goswamiReproducibleOrchestration2026`@@. Finite-temperature kinetics additionally requires finite-timestep propagation, thermal velocity seeding, dividing-surface velocity boundary rules, deterministic PRNG reseeding, and holonomic hyperplane constraints.

=rgflux::engine::DynamicsEngine= captures that contract in an object-safe trait. Every trajectory sampler in =rgflux= accepts =&mut E= for =E: DynamicsEngine + ?Sized=, supporting both monomorphized generics and =&mut dyn DynamicsEngine= dynamic dispatch without coupling rate estimators to a particular molecular dynamics package @@rst::cite:`goswamiEfficientExploration2025,eon2901`@@.

** How each engine backend is wired

1. *In-process BAOAB + RATTLE/SHAKE reference backend (=engine::native::NativeEngine=)*:
   Integrates unconstrained or hyperplane-constrained Langevin dynamics with white noise (\(\gamma > 0\)) or the smart-sampling colored-noise GLE, and velocity-Verlet NVE dynamics when white noise uses \(\gamma = 0\), through the symmetric BAOAB splitting (half velocity kick *B*, half coordinate drift *A*, exact Ornstein-Uhlenbeck thermostat update *O*, half drift *A*, closing half kick *B*) @@rst::cite:`leimkuhlerRationalConstruction2012,leimkuhlerGeodesicIntegration2016`@@. The GLE supplies its own drift and remains canonical when \(\gamma = 0\). Static potential energy surfaces enter through =engine::ForceSurface=, which converts gradients \(+\nabla V(\mathbf{x})\) into physical forces \(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}) = -\nabla V(\mathbf{x})\):
   - Any =rgsaddle::PointSurface= via a blanket =ForceSurface= implementation (forwarding optional periodic cells through =eval_in_cell=).
   - Any =eindir_core::DifferentiableObjective<f64>= (as used by =rgmin::Oracle=) wrapped in =engine::EindirSurface=.
   - Any =rgpot_core::potential::PotentialImpl= callback wrapped in =engine::rgpot::RgpotSurface= (feature =rgpot=), exchanging zero-copy CPU DLPack tensors (=rgpot_force_input_t= / =rgpot_force_out_t=).
2. *Direct LAMMPS backend (=engine::lammpc::LammpcEngine=, feature =lammpc=)*:
   Links =liblammpc= and drives its persistent =lammpc_session_*= C ABI with =PotentialConfig= and =ForceInput= Cap'n Proto messages from =rgpot-core=. It supports constrained white-noise Langevin (\(\gamma > 0\)) or zero-friction projected velocity-Verlet (\(\gamma = 0\)) dynamics on a =HyperplaneConstraint= via =lammpc_session_md_initialize=, =lammpc_session_md_step=, and =lammpc_session_md_state= (evaluating endpoint potential energy and physical forces via =lammpc_session_calculate_result= to form the mass-weighted multiplier \(\lambda\)), as well as unconstrained velocity-Verlet NVE (\(\gamma = 0\), no constraint) via =lammpc_session_nve_*= with periodic image-flag coordinate unwrapping. Colored-noise selections return =FluxError::InvalidConfig= instead of being silently treated as white noise. Unconstrained Langevin (\(\gamma > 0\), no constraint) returns =FluxError::BackendUnavailable=, and C ABI failures return =FluxError::Lammpc=.
3. *External MD session adapter (=engine::rgpot_session::RgpotSessionEngine=, feature =rgpot-session=)*:
   Targets =rgpot='s engine-neutral session C ABI (=rgpot_md_session_*= and =rgpot_core::md_session=) across OpenMM (=SessionEngineKind::OpenMm=, ="openmm"=), GROMACS =nblib= (=SessionEngineKind::NbLib=, ="nblib"=), and LAMMPS (=SessionEngineKind::Lammps=, ="lammps"=). State accessors, Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity draws, and mass-weighted Householder dividing-surface reflections operate in-process; on =develop=, =step=, =energy_and_forces=, and =reseed= return =FluxError::BackendUnavailable= until =rgpot_core::md_session= merges onto =rgpot= =develop=.

** Units across engines and estimators

=rgflux= is unit-consistent with the active =DynamicsEngine=:

- Let \([L]\), \([M]\), \([E]\), and \([T]\) denote the engine's length, mass, energy, and time units (satisfying \([E] = [M][L]^2[T]^{-2}\) in consistent dynamical units, or reduced units where \(k_{\mathrm{B}} = 1\)).
- Hyperplane normals \(\mathbf{n}\) are dimensionless Euclidean unit vectors (\(\|\mathbf{n}\|_2 = 1\) within =UNIT_NORMAL_TOL= \(= 64\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}\) @@rst::cite:`highamAccuracyStability2002`@@); plane offsets \(c\) have unit \([L]\).
- Potential energies \(V(\mathbf{x})\), kinetic energies \(K(\mathbf{v}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_i m_i v_i^2\), and thermal energy \(k_{\mathrm{B}} T\) (=ThermostatConfig::thermal_energy=) have unit \([E]\).
- Physical forces \(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}) = -\nabla V(\mathbf{x})\) and the holonomic constraint Lagrange multiplier \(\lambda = dA/dc\) have unit \([E][L]^{-1}\).
- First-order transition rates (\(k_{\mathrm{OHTST}}\) in =ohtst=, \(k_{AB}\) in =ffs=, and =normalized_rate= in =pafi=) have inverse time units \([T]^{-1}\).
- Across the =lammpc=, =rgpot=, and =rgpot-session= boundaries, quantities follow LAMMPS =metal= / =rgpot= units: coordinates and box vectors in Å, velocities in Å/ps, forces in eV/Å, energies and \(k_{\mathrm{B}}T\) in eV, masses in amu (with dynamical conversion factor =MASS_VELOCITY_TO_EV= \(= 1.0364269656262175 \times 10^{-4}\;\mathrm{eV / (amu \cdot \AA^2 / ps^2)}\) in =LammpcEngine=), time steps in ps, and Langevin friction \(\gamma\) in \(\mathrm{ps}^{-1}\).

* Holonomic hyperplane sampling

** Theory

Optimal Hyperplanar Transition State Theory (OH-TST) @@rst::cite:`goswamiEfficientExploration2025,goswamiAdaptivePruning2026,goswamiEnhancedClimbingImage2026,johannessonOptimizationHyperplanar2001,bligaardOptimizationHyperplanar2005`@@ approximates the dividing surface between reactant and product basins by a hyperplane

\[
\sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{x} - c = 0, \qquad \|\mathbf{n}\|_2 = 1,
\]

with constrained Helmholtz free energy

\[
A(\mathbf{n}, c) = -k_{\mathrm{B}} T \ln \int \delta(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{x} - c)\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{V(\mathbf{x})}{k_{\mathrm{B}} T}\right) d\mathbf{x}.
\]

Under diagonal mass matrix \(\mathbf{M} = \mathrm{diag}(m_i)\), the inverse-mass metric norm of \(\nabla \sigma = \mathbf{n}\) is

\[
z = \mathbf{n}^\top \mathbf{M}^{-1} \mathbf{n} = \sum_{i=1}^{D} \frac{n_i^2}{m_i}, \qquad \mu = \frac{1}{z} = \left(\sum_{i=1}^{D} \frac{n_i^2}{m_i}\right)^{-1},
\]

where \(\mu\) is the effective directional flux mass along \(\mathbf{n}\), evaluated via =rgsaddle::flux_mass=. Note that Eq. (24) of Jóhannesson and Jónsson (2001) @@rst::cite:`johannessonOptimizationHyperplanar2001`@@ writes \(\sum_i m_i n_i^2\), which coincides with \(\mu = (\sum_i n_i^2 / m_i)^{-1}\) only when all coordinates share a common mass. Because \(z\) is independent of position on a flat hyperplane, the Blue Moon geometric Jacobian of Sprik and Ciccotti (1998) @@rst::cite:`sprikFreeEnergyConstrained1998`@@ is constant across \(\sigma(\mathbf{x}) = 0\), and the thermodynamic mean force equals the canonical average of the holonomic Lagrange multiplier:

\[
\frac{\partial A}{\partial c} = \langle \lambda \rangle_c, \qquad \lambda(\mathbf{x}) = -\frac{\mathbf{n}^\top \mathbf{M}^{-1} \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})}{\mathbf{n}^\top \mathbf{M}^{-1} \mathbf{n}} = \frac{\mathbf{n}^\top \mathbf{M}^{-1} \nabla V(\mathbf{x})}{\mathbf{n}^\top \mathbf{M}^{-1} \mathbf{n}}.
\]

=hyperplane::sample_hyperplane= equilibrates the constrained system for =burn_in_steps=, records \(\lambda_t\) over =sample_steps= steps, and estimates \(\langle \lambda \rangle_c\) and its block-averaged standard error \(\sigma_\lambda\) via =stats::batch_means= @@rst::cite:`flyvbjergErrorEstimates1989,flegalMcmcThirdSignificantFigure2008`@@ while retaining dividing-surface configurations every =retention_stride= steps. The average is a canonical expectation only when the dynamics samples the canonical ensemble, so =sample_hyperplane= requires a supported canonical thermostat. White noise requires \(\gamma > 0\); the smart-sampling GLE remains thermostatted at \(\gamma = 0\) on engines that implement it. NVE dynamics started from the anchor samples an energy shell and returns =FluxError::InvalidConfig=; the mean it gave before this check was biased with no error. In the canonical regime the mean does not depend on \(\gamma\), the constraint scheme (=ConstraintProjection::Rattle= or =Shake=), or the mass distribution, only its standard error and the burn-in it needs do.

** Assumptions and scope

- Positive thermal energy \(k_{\mathrm{B}} T > 0\), a supported canonical thermostat, and strictly positive masses \(m_i > 0\). White noise needs \(\gamma > 0\).

** Engine wiring

=sample_hyperplane= captures an internal =EngineSnapshot= (=positions=, =velocities=, =cell=, =thermostat=, and =constraint=) before modifying the engine and restores those five fields on exit (returning any restoration error if sampling succeeded, or preserving the primary sampling error if both failed). In =NativeEngine= and =LammpcEngine=, two-pass linear SHAKE projects positions to relative residual \(\lvert \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{x} - c \rvert / (1 + \lvert c \rvert + \|\mathbf{x}\|_2) \le 16\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}\) (=HYPERPLANE_PROJECTION_TOL=) @@rst::cite:`ryckaertShake1977`@@, and RATTLE projects velocities to the mass-metric tangent space \(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0\) @@rst::cite:`andersenRattle1983,leimkuhlerGeodesicIntegration2016`@@.

* Optimal hyperplanar transition state theory (=ohtst=)

The dividing surface is a hyperplane with point \(\mathbf{p}\) and unit normal \(\boldsymbol{\nu}\) in mass-weighted coordinates, and its free energy is \(A(\mathbf{p}, \boldsymbol{\nu}) = -k_{\mathrm{B}} T \ln \int \delta(\boldsymbol{\nu} \cdot (\mathbf{q} - \mathbf{p}))\, e^{-V(\mathbf{q}) / k_{\mathrm{B}} T}\, d\mathbf{q}\). The plane that maximizes \(A\) gives the smallest hyperplanar transition state rate @@rst::cite:`johannessonOptimizationHyperplanar2001`@@. =ohtst::PlaneOptimizer= climbs \(A\) in both the offset and the orientation, =ohtst::basin_density= gives \(Z_H / Z_R\) on a plane through the minimum, =ohtst::reversible_work= gives \(\Delta A\) from that plane to the optimum, and =ohtst::ohtst_rate= returns \(k = \sqrt{k_{\mathrm{B}} T / 2\pi}\,(Z_H / Z_R)\, e^{-\Delta A / k_{\mathrm{B}} T}\). =ohtst::transmission_coefficient= shoots from the optimal plane for the recrossing factor. Surfaces enter as =rgsaddle::PointSurface=, wrapped in =rgsaddle::MassWeighted= when they are Cartesian.

* Forward Flux Sampling (FFS)

** Theory

Direct Forward Flux Sampling @@rst::cite:`goswamiEfficientExploration2025,allenSamplingRareSwitching2005,allenSimulatingRareEvents2006,allenForwardFluxEfficiency2006,allenForwardFluxReview2009`@@ computes the rare-event transition rate \(k_{AB}\) from reactant basin \(A\) (\(\lambda(\mathbf{x}) \le \lambda_0\)) to product state \(B\) (\(\lambda(\mathbf{x}) \ge \lambda_n\)) across strictly ordered reaction-coordinate interfaces \(\lambda_0 < \lambda_1 < \dots < \lambda_n\):

\[
k_{AB} = \Phi_{A,0} \prod_{i=0}^{n-1} P(\lambda_{i+1} \mid \lambda_i).
\]

=ffs::forward_flux_rate= evaluates this product in two phases:

1. *Initial escape flux \(\Phi_{A,0}\)*: Starting from an initial state inside basin \(A\) (\(\lambda(\mathbf{x}_0) \le \lambda_0\)), the engine is reseeded from =config.seed=, =burn_in_steps= steps are discarded, and the trajectory is stepped by \(\Delta t = \text{time\_step}\) up to =max_flux_steps= until \(M_0 = \text{points\_per\_interface} \ge 2\) positive-going crossings of \(\lambda_0\) are collected. The crossing detector is armed whenever \(\lambda(\mathbf{x}) \le \lambda_0\) and triggers on the first subsequent step with \(\lambda(\mathbf{x}) > \lambda_0\), storing the phase point \((\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v})\) and the elapsed waiting time \(\Delta \tau_m > 0\) since the previous crossing (or since burn-in finished). If an excursion reaches the product level \(\lambda(\mathbf{x}) \ge \lambda_n\), the engine is immediately reset to the initial basin-\(A\) phase point \((\mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{v}_0)\) and re-armed. From the batch-means average waiting interval \(\bar{\tau}\) and standard error \(\sigma_{\bar{\tau}}\) (=stats::batch_means=), the escape flux and its delta-method uncertainty are

   \[
   \Phi_{A,0} = \frac{1}{\bar{\tau}}, \qquad \sigma_{\Phi_{A,0}} = \frac{\sigma_{\bar{\tau}}}{\bar{\tau}^2}.
   \]

2. *Staged conditional probabilities \(P(\lambda_{i+1} \mid \lambda_i)\)*: At each stage \(i = 0, \dots, n - 1\) with \(M_i \ge 2\) stored phase points at \(\lambda_i\), \(K = \text{trials\_per\_interface} \ge 1\) trial trajectories are launched by drawing a starting index \(c \in \{0, \dots, M_i - 1\}\) uniformly, restoring its \((\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v})\), calling =engine.reseed(seed)= with a fresh deterministic seed from the =StdRng= stream, and stepping up to =max_trial_steps= until \(\lambda(\mathbf{x}) \ge \lambda_{i+1}\) (success; phase point saved for stage \(i + 1\) when \(i + 1 < n - 1\)) or \(\lambda(\mathbf{x}) \le \lambda_0\) (return to basin \(A\), failure). With \(k_c\) trials and \(s_c\) successes launched from starting point \(c\) (\(\sum_c k_c = K\), \(\hat{p}_i = \frac{1}{K}\sum_c s_c\)), the cluster-variance standard error captures heterogeneity across entry points on \(\lambda_i\):

   \[
   \sigma_{p_i} = \frac{1}{K}\sqrt{\frac{M_i}{M_i - 1}\sum_{c=0}^{M_i - 1} (s_c - \hat{p}_i k_c)^2}.
   \]

3. *Composite rate and error propagation*: Combining the initial flux and stage probabilities in quadrature yields

   \[
   \sigma_{k_{AB}} = k_{AB}\sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_{\Phi_{A,0}}}{\Phi_{A,0}}\right)^2 + \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \left(\frac{\sigma_{p_i}}{\hat{p}_i}\right)^2}.
   \]

** Assumptions and units

- Does *not* require detailed balance or knowledge of the stationary phase-space distribution, making it applicable to both equilibrium and driven non-equilibrium stochastic systems @@rst::cite:`allenSimulatingRareEvents2006`@@.
- Requires stochastic dynamics (e.g. Langevin \(\gamma > 0\)) so that trajectories launched from the same interface phase point \((\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v})\) diverge after =DynamicsEngine::reseed=.
- Requires strictly increasing interfaces \(\lambda_0 < \lambda_1 < \dots < \lambda_n\) (\(n + 1 \ge 2\)), initial state \(\lambda(\mathbf{x}_0) \le \lambda_0\), at least two collected phase points per interface (\(M_i \ge 2\)), and at least one successful trial per stage (\(\hat{p}_i > 0\)).
- Order parameter \(\lambda(\mathbf{x})\) has any user-chosen unit; \(\Phi_{A,0}\), \(k_{AB}\), and \(\sigma_{k_{AB}}\) have unit \([T]^{-1}\); \(P(\lambda_{i+1} \mid \lambda_i)\) is dimensionless.

** Engine wiring

=forward_flux_rate= drives any =DynamicsEngine= via =positions=, =velocities=, =set_positions=, =set_velocities=, =reseed=, and =step=. On entry it captures an =EngineSnapshot= (=positions=, =velocities=, =cell=, =thermostat=, and =constraint=) and writes all five pieces back before returning (returning any restoration error when the run succeeded, or preserving the run error if both failed). The stochastic stream is left where the flux and trial =reseed= calls placed it.

* Projected Average Force Integration (PAFI)

** Theory

Projected Average Force Integration (PAFI) @@rst::cite:`goswamiEfficientExploration2025,goswamiEnhancedClimbingImage2026,goswamiReproducibleOrchestration2026,swinburneUnsupervisedCalculation2018`@@ turns a converged minimum-energy path \(\mathbf{X}(s)\), for example a nudged elastic band, into a finite-temperature free-energy profile \(F(s)\) without a global collective variable. The method itself is that of Swinburne and Marinica; the paths it needs come from the band methods cited first.

The path parameter \(s\) is the arc length in the mass-weighted metric, \(ds^2 = d\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{M}\, d\mathbf{X}\) with \(\mathbf{M} = \mathrm{diag}(m_i)\). At each \(s\) the plane has the unit normal \(\mathbf{n}(s) = \mathbf{M}\mathbf{X}'(s) / \lVert \mathbf{M}\mathbf{X}'(s) \rVert\) and the equation \(\sigma(\mathbf{x}, s) = \mathbf{n}(s) \cdot (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{X}(s)) = 0\), so planes are orthogonal to the path in the metric \(\mathbf{M}\). Neighbouring planes tilt at the rate \(\mathbf{n}' = d\mathbf{n}/ds\), which adds a Jacobian term to the constrained mean force. With \(\lambda\) the Lagrange multiplier of the holonomic constraint on the plane (the same multiplier as in the hyperplane section above):

\[
\frac{dF}{ds}(s) = \Bigl\langle \lambda(\mathbf{x}) \bigl( \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{X}' - (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{X}) \cdot \mathbf{n}' \bigr) \Bigr\rangle_{\sigma(\mathbf{x}, s) = 0}.
\]

Two limits anchor the estimator:

- *Straight path (\(\mathbf{n}' = \mathbf{0}\))*: the Jacobian term vanishes and \(dF/ds\) is the Sprik-Ciccotti mean constraint force \(\langle \lambda \rangle\) that =hyperplane::sample_hyperplane= samples on a single plane @@rst::cite:`sprikFreeEnergyConstrained1998`@@. On the widening double well \(V(x, y) = (1 - x^2)^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}k(x)y^2\) with \(k(x) = 1.5 + 0.5x\), the profile from \(x = -1\) to \(x = 0\) ends at \(\Delta F(T) = \Delta E_0 + \tfrac{1}{2}k_{\mathrm{B}}T \ln(k(0)/k(-1))\). The integration tests reproduce this value within two standard errors, for unit and anisotropic masses.
- *Zero temperature (\(T \to 0\))*: the sampled points collapse onto the anchor and the integral of \(dF/ds\) becomes the static line integral \(\int \nabla V(\mathbf{X}) \cdot \mathbf{X}'\,ds = \Delta E_0\). =pafi_profile= evaluates that trapezoid sum on the plane anchors before it samples anything and compares it with the end-to-end energy change, see the tolerance note below.

The profile integrates the plane means \(g_k\) with the cubic Hermite rule. On an interval of width \(h\) with endpoint derivatives \(g'\) from a five-point Lagrange window,

\[
\int_{s_k}^{s_{k+1}} g\,ds = \tfrac{h}{2}(g_k + g_{k+1}) + \tfrac{h^2}{12}(g'_k - g'_{k+1}).
\]

With two planes the derivative correction cancels and the rule is the trapezoid rule, exact for a linear gradient. With three planes it is exact for a quadratic gradient. With four or more planes the window carries a cubic gradient exactly. The barrier \(\Delta F\) is the global maximum of that interpolant, searched on every interval, so it can sit between samples and away from the highest sample. The reactant partition function \(Z_R = 2\int_0^{s^*} \exp(-(F(s) - F(s_0)) / k_{\mathrm{B}}T)\,ds\) is evaluated by the doubled-trapezoid rule over the planes up to the dividing segment (through \(s_k\) when \(s^* = s_k\) and through \(s_{k+1}\) when \(s^*\) lies strictly inside \([s_k, s_{k+1}]\)), and the normalized rate is

\[
k = \sqrt{\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{2\pi}}\,\frac{\exp(-\Delta F / k_{\mathrm{B}}T)}{Z_R}.
\]

The reactant curvature \(\omega_0 = (\mathbf{n}^\top \mathbf{H}^{-1} \mathbf{n})^{-1/2}\) at the first image is reported in =reactant_frequency= for diagnostics and comparison with Vineyard harmonic transition-state theory; it does not enter \(k\). \(\ln Z_R\) and \(\ln k\) are stored in =reactant_log_partition= and =normalized_log_rate=, so a large barrier does not underflow the reported log rate. The linear fields =reactant_partition=, =normalized_rate=, and =normalized_rate_error= are =Option<f64>=. =reactant_partition= and =normalized_rate= are =None= when \(\exp(\cdot)\) of a finite log overflows or underflows =f64=, because a partition or a rate of exactly zero would be read as a true zero (and the partition divides the rate), whereas the log fields keep the value. =normalized_rate_error= is =None= only when the linear error overflows, and is =Some(0.0)= when it underflows, since an absolute error that rounds to zero is negligible next to any representable rate.

What \(k\) is: the transition-state rate of the one-dimensional reduction along the path, with the dividing plane at the maximum of the profile and the reactant partition function \(Z_R\) given by the doubled trapezoid above. It assumes a canonical reactant population, no recrossing (transmission coefficient 1), and a reactant well that the sampled planes between the first image and the dividing plane resolve. For a reactant well that is harmonic along the path normal over the thermal range it approaches Vineyard harmonic transition-state theory, \((\omega_0 / 2\pi)\exp(-\Delta F / k_{\mathrm{B}}T)\), with the transverse entropy already in \(\Delta F\).

What \(k\) is not: it is not a dynamical rate, because recrossing is not estimated, and it is not the Gaussian-reactant rate. On an anharmonic reactant well the doubled trapezoid differs from the Gaussian \(\sqrt{2\pi k_{\mathrm{B}}T} / \omega_0\), and \(k\) follows the integral. \(\Delta F\) is the Hermite integral of the sampled plane means and approaches \(F(s^\ddagger) - F(s_0)\) of the plane foliation as the sampling and the plane spacing are refined.

** What =pafi_profile= does

1. *Spline*: The images become a cubic spline in the chord-length parameter, the sum of \(\sqrt{\sum_j m_j \Delta x_j^2}\) between neighbours. The end second derivatives follow from linear extrapolation of the second derivative, the not-a-knot condition, so the first and last planes keep the curvature of the path. Natural end conditions (zero second derivative) would set \(\mathbf{n}'\) to zero on the end planes and bias the end increments of the profile. Three images give one parabola, two images a straight line. Coincident images are rejected.
2. *Planes*: =planes= planes sit at equal values of the parameter from the first to the last image. Each has an anchor \(\mathbf{X}(s_k)\), the normal \(\mathbf{n}\), and \(\mathbf{n}'\).
3. *Zero-temperature check*: let \(I_T\) be the trapezoid sum of \(dV/ds\) over the anchors and \(\Delta E_0 = V(\mathbf{X}(s_{K-1})) - V(\mathbf{X}(s_0))\). The code compares \(\lvert I_T - \Delta E_0\rvert / \max(E_{\mathrm{unit}}, \lvert\Delta E_0\rvert)\) with the effective =zero_temperature_tol=, where \(E_{\mathrm{unit}}\) is one energy unit of the engine. A mismatch returns =FluxError::InvalidConfig= without sampling.
4. *Sampling*: On each plane the engine gets the constraint, is placed at the anchor, receives Maxwell-Boltzmann velocities from the per-plane seed, runs =burn_in_steps= steps, and then =sample_steps= steps that each record \(\lambda\,(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{X}' - (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{X}) \cdot \mathbf{n}')\). =stats::batch_means= turns each series into a mean and a standard error.
5. *Integration*: The profile and the dividing plane follow from the Hermite rule on the plane means. At the first image, forces must be finite. The mass-weighted Hessian is checked for positive definiteness off the zero modes by a dense symmetric eigensolver when the coordinate count is at most 128 (\(\text{dof} \le 128\)); above that limit the dense check is skipped and =reactant_hessian_certified= is =false=. At every size, \(\omega_0\) is evaluated from conjugate gradients on central-difference Hessian-vector products of =energy_and_forces=, so non-positive projected curvature is still rejected. The plane errors are independent and propagate in log space through the Hermite weights of the profile and barrier (combined by =hypot= with the Hermite-minus-trapezoid truncation residual) and through the sensitivity of \(\ln k = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln(k_{\mathrm{B}}T / 2\pi) - \Delta F / k_{\mathrm{B}}T - \ln Z_R\) to each plane mean.
6. *Adaptive rounds*: With =target_error= set, steps 4 and 5 repeat for up to =max_rounds= rounds, each adding =sample_steps= samples to every plane, until =barrier_error= is at most the target. The result uses all samples. Missing the target is not an error, so compare =barrier_error= with it.

The seed of plane \(k\) in round \(r\) is =seed + 4099 * k + 1000003 * r= (wrapping), so the planes are independent and a run is reproducible.

** Zero-temperature tolerance

The numerator and denominator in step 3 are energies in the engine's energy units, so their ratio and =zero_temperature_tol= are dimensionless. For \(\lvert\Delta E_0\rvert \ge E_{\mathrm{unit}}\) the check is relative to the endpoint energy change. Below one energy unit it instead bounds the absolute discrepancy to the effective tolerance times one engine energy unit.

=None=, the value stored by =PafiConfig::default()=, makes the effective tolerance \(2 (\pi^2 / 12) / (\text{planes} - 1)^2\), which is \(2 (\pi^2 / 12) / 8^2 \approx 0.0257\) at the default 9 planes. Changing =planes= scales that tolerance. An explicit value passed to =with_zero_temperature_tol= is the tolerance at every plane count, including when it equals the 9-plane value. This scaling follows the trapezoid error on a quarter-turn circular arc through a harmonic well, \(\pi^2 / (12 (\text{planes} - 1)^2)\). A path that turns through more than a quarter circle can need a larger explicit tolerance, which also weakens the guard against inconsistent forces and tangents.

** Assumptions, units, and engine wiring

- Assumes a smooth path of at least two images, with one coordinate per degree of freedom of the engine, whose thermal fluctuations on each plane stay inside the region where neighbouring planes do not intersect, so that \(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{X}' - (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{X}) \cdot \mathbf{n}'\) stays positive.
- The plane means are canonical averages, so the engine must run a supported canonical thermostat at positive \(k_{\mathrm{B}}T\). White noise requires \(\gamma > 0\), and =pafi_profile= returns =FluxError::InvalidConfig= for its zero-friction NVE mode. A supported colored-noise thermostat can remain canonical at \(\gamma = 0\). In a canonical regime the expected profile does not depend on \(\gamma\); a larger white-noise \(\gamma\) changes the standard error and the burn-in, and a time step too large for \(\gamma\) and the stiffest mode adds an \(O(\Delta t^2)\) bias.
- Every plane starts at its anchor, so a plane whose thermal centre lies far from the anchor needs a long =burn_in_steps=. The first image represents the reactant basin: its forces must be finite, its mass-weighted Hessian off the zero modes must be positive definite when dense certification is performed at \(\text{dof} \le 128\), and its normal curvature must be positive at every size. Above 128 coordinates dense certification is skipped rather than rejected, and the returned =reactant_hessian_certified= diagnostic is =false=.
- Path images, masses and positions use the engine units \([L]\) and \([M]\); the arc length \(s\) is mass-weighted, \([M]^{1/2}[L]\), and equals a length for unit masses. =free_energy=, =standard_error=, =barrier=, =barrier_error=, =target_error=, =quadrature_residual= and \(k_{\mathrm{B}}T\) are in \([E]\), =time_step= in \([T]\), =reactant_frequency=, =reactant_frequency_error= and =normalized_rate= in \([T]^{-1}\), and =dividing_arc= in the units of \(s\). For Cartesian systems with at least two atoms, the reactant Hessian is probed along each mass-weighted rigid translation and only numerically verified zero modes are projected. Tethered or externally confined translations remain in the curvature, while a path tangent lying entirely in a true translational zero-mode subspace is rejected explicitly. One-dimensional analytic models and single-particle Cartesian confinement are left unprojected.
- =pafi_profile= accepts any =E: DynamicsEngine + ?Sized=, and =pafi_profile_dyn= takes =&mut dyn DynamicsEngine=. The engine must report =StepReport::constraint_force= on constrained steps and implement =energy_and_forces= for the zero-temperature check and for the reactant Hessian.
- Like the other samplers it restores positions, velocities, the periodic cell, the thermostat, and the constraint on return, including on errors. The stochastic stream is left where the last plane reseeded it.
- For adaptive plane placement, =EquidistributionMap= builds the monotone reparametrization \(s \mapsto \sigma(s) \in [0, 1]\) that equidistributes a positive monitor function (\(\lvert dF/ds\rvert\), path normal curvature \(\lVert d\mathbf{n}/ds\rVert_2\), \(\tau_{\mathrm{int}}\)-weighted constraint-force variance, or their normalized combination =MonitorKind::Combined=) with a defensive-mixture floor equal to the path-average monitor \(\bar{w} = \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L w(s)\,ds\) so planes never collapse @@rst::cite:`deBoorVariableKnots1973`@@. =estimate_spectral_tail= fits a Chebyshev / cosine expansion to the mean constraint force and integrated free energy on the current planes to compute a heuristic resolution indicator and flag under-resolved intervals. It is not a rigorous upper bound on interpolation error. =pafi_profile_adaptive= iterates placement, sampling, spectral fitting, and plane insertion until this indicator is at or below the statistical standard error of \(F^*\).
- Adaptive placement is not guaranteed to reduce force calls. In the deterministic regression cases, the localized symmetric double well uses 15 adaptive planes and 670 force calls versus 27 uniform planes and 329 calls at matched barrier error; the asymmetric barrier uses 15 adaptive planes, 670 calls and \(6.45\times10^{-3}\) error versus 10 uniform planes, 125 calls and \(1.01\times10^{-3}\) error. These examples establish placement behavior and tradeoffs, not universal efficiency.
- When replicates are collected on a shared plane grid (such as in the FCC Cu EAM vacancy benchmark @@rst::cite:`mishinCopperEam2001,sivakCopperDefectDiffusion2021`@@), =pool_pafi_replicates= pools the per-plane mean constraint forces across replicates before integrating \(F(s)\) and evaluating \(\exp(-F(s^*)/k_{\mathrm{B}}T) / Z_R\), avoiding the positive Jensen bias \(\mathbb{E}[\exp(-F^*/k_{\mathrm{B}}T)] > \exp(-\mathbb{E}[F^*]/k_{\mathrm{B}}T)\) of averaging per-replicate rates. Its delta-method rate error combines the pooled barrier uncertainty and between-replicate reactant-frequency uncertainty in quadrature.

** Displacement between two atom groups

=group_displacement_scan= fixes a different coordinate on a cell that the scan does not change. For atom groups \(A\) and \(B\) with masses \(M_A\) and \(M_B\) and mass-weighted centres \(\mathbf{R}_A\) and \(\mathbf{R}_B\),

\[
s = \mathbf{n}\cdot(\mathbf{R}_B - \mathbf{R}_A),
\]

where \(\mathbf{n}\) is the supplied direction after normalisation. The Cartesian gradient is \((m_i/M_B)\mathbf{n}\) on an atom of \(B\), \(-(m_i/M_A)\mathbf{n}\) on an atom of \(A\), and zero on every other atom. A uniform translation cancels. The existing hyperplane sampler constrains the unit vector \(\boldsymbol{\nu} = \nabla s / \lVert\nabla s\rVert_2\) at offset \(s / \lVert\nabla s\rVert_2\). Its multiplier satisfies \(\langle\lambda\rangle = dF/dc\), and the scan reports

\[
\frac{dF}{ds} = \frac{\langle\lambda\rangle}{\lVert\nabla s\rVert_2}.
\]

The sign is the Sprik-Ciccotti sign: \(\lambda\) is positive when the potential increases with \(s\). The window values of \(dF/ds\) are integrated with the same cubic Hermite rule as the path profile. A periodic cell keeps one reference per window, \(\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{ref}} = s\mathbf{n}\), and replaces \(\mathbf{R}_B-\mathbf{R}_A\) by \(\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{ref}}\) plus the minimum image of the residual, using the restricted triclinic unwrapping of the engine cell. Atoms of one rigid constraint group must lie wholly in \(A\), wholly in \(B\), or wholly in neither.

* Reactive-flux recrossing (=kappa= and =kappa::bennett_chandler=)

Transition state theory overcounts the true transition rate whenever trajectories recross the dividing surface before thermalizing. In the Bennett-Chandler reactive-flux formalism @@rst::cite:`goswamiEfficientExploration2025,goswamiAdaptivePruning2026,bennettMolecularDynamics1977,chandlerStatisticalMechanics1978,andersonStatisticalTheories1973,vanErpNovelPath2003`@@, trajectories launched from the dividing surface \(\sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{x} - c = 0\) with initial normal velocity \(v_n(0) = \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v}(0)\) and post-launch side indicator \(\theta(s(t)) \in \{0, 1\}\) define the time-dependent transmission coefficient

\[
\kappa(t) = \frac{\langle v_n(0)\,\theta(s(t)) \rangle}{\langle v_n(0)\,\theta(v_n(0)) \rangle}.
\]

- =kappa::bennett_chandler::estimate_bennett_chandler= is completely engine-free: it evaluates \(\kappa(t)\) at every step over recorded =ReactiveTrajectory= slices, tests stationarity across the plateau window \([\lfloor f_{\mathrm{start}} T \rfloor, \lceil f_{\mathrm{end}} T \rceil)\) combining temporal batch-means variance with paired trajectory-ensemble early-minus-late shift variance, and computes the plateau standard error from linearized ratio influence values (automatically pairing antithetic \((+\mathbf{v}, -\mathbf{v})\) shots into even batch widths). Values of \(\kappa(t)\) and the plateau are never clamped into \([0, 1]\).
- =kappa::bennett_chandler::shoot_reactive_trajectories= and =shoot_bennett_chandler= clear any active hyperplane constraint on a =DynamicsEngine=, shoot trajectories (including antithetic pairs with independent Langevin noise streams when =ShootingConfig::antithetic= is =true=) from dividing-surface configurations retained by =sample_hyperplane=, restore the engine's constraint, and reduce the ensemble via =estimate_bennett_chandler=. Fixed-length shooting is the default: =KappaConfig::commit_checkpoints = 0= integrates every half to =ShootingConfig::steps= and preserves the full side history. Early commitment is an opt-in optimization. Set a nonzero checkpoint count no greater than =steps= and a strictly positive =KappaConfig::commitment_distance=. Every configuration requires a finite =commitment_distance=. A checkpoint counts toward product commitment only when \(s \ge \text{commitment\_distance}\), toward reactant commitment only when \(s \le -\text{commitment\_distance}\), and otherwise resets the streak. Once the configured number of consecutive checkpoints agrees, that side fills the remaining history. This is safe only when the distance lies inside a physically defined thermalized basin and the window exceeds every relevant recrossing time (for a parabolic Langevin barrier, a conservative scale is several \(1/\lambda_+\), \(\lambda_+ = \sqrt{\gamma^2/4 + \omega_b^2} - \gamma/2\)). A signed-distance threshold is not a proof of irreversible commitment: a trajectory can penetrate deeply and return later, so uncertain systems must retain fixed-length shooting. Steepest-descent endpoint quenching is not yet implemented, so =KappaConfig::quench_force_tol= and =quench_max_steps= remain reserved.
- =kappa::kramers_transmission_coefficient= evaluates Kramers' analytical spatial-diffusion barrier-recrossing factor across a parabolic barrier of unstable frequency \(\omega_b > 0\) under Langevin friction \(\gamma \ge 0\) @@rst::cite:`kramersBrownianMotion1940`@@:

  \[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{K}} = \sqrt{1 + \alpha^2} - \alpha = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \alpha^2} + \alpha}, \qquad \alpha = \frac{\gamma}{2 \omega_b}.
  \]

* Correlated time-series diagnostics (=stats=)

Correlated MD and Markov-chain observables underestimate uncertainty when treated as independent draws @@rst::cite:`goswamiBayesianHierarchical2025,flyvbjergErrorEstimates1989`@@:

- =stats::effective_sample_size= estimates the integrated autocorrelation time \(\tau_{\mathrm{int}}\) and statistical inefficiency \(g = 2\tau_{\mathrm{int}} \ge 1\) via Geyer's initial positive and monotone sequence estimator @@rst::cite:`geyerPracticalMcmc1992`@@, returning \(n_{\mathrm{eff}} = n / g \in [1, n]\).
- =stats::batch_means= partitions a series of length \(n\) into \(a\) non-overlapping batches of width \(b\) (automatically choosing \(\max(\lceil 2\tau_{\mathrm{int}} \rceil, \lfloor \sqrt{n} \rfloor)\) subject to =min_batches= when =batch_size= is =None=) @@rst::cite:`flegalMcmcThirdSignificantFigure2008`@@. Series whose spread from the first sample lies within \(\text{SERIES\_FLAT\_TOL}\,(1 + |x_0|)\) return \(x_0\) directly with zero standard error so repeated non-dyadic IEEE-754 floats are never rounded to a neighboring value by summation.
- =stats::check_stationarity= compares the leading =stationarity_first_fraction= (\(0.25\)) and trailing =stationarity_last_fraction= (\(0.50\)) sub-windows via Geweke's two-window \(z\)-score @@rst::cite:`gewekeEvaluatingAccuracy1992,choderaAutomatedEquilibration2016`@@ alongside the ordinary-least-squares linear drift slope.

* Multithermal OPES (=opes=)

** Theory

On-the-fly probability enhanced sampling with an expanded-ensemble target @@rst::cite:`invernizziUnifiedApproach2020,invernizziRethinkingMetadynamics2020`@@ builds a bias whose stationary distribution is a uniform mixture of ensembles. In the multithermal case the ensembles are canonical at \(T_1 \le \dots \le T_N\), the run is thermostatted at \(T_0\), and \(\beta = 1 / (k_{\mathrm{B}} T)\). The expansion collective variables are

\[
\Delta u_k(\mathbf{x}) = (\beta_k - \beta_0)\, U(\mathbf{x}),
\]

with \(U\) the potential energy. After \(n\) updates the bias is

\[
V_n(U) = -\frac{1}{\beta_0} \ln \Biggl[ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^{N} \exp\bigl(-\Delta u_k + \beta_0 \Delta F_n(k)\bigr) \Biggr],
\]

and the free-energy differences are the reweighted estimates over the samples taken every =pace= steps,

\[
e^{-\beta_0 \Delta F_n(k)} = \frac{\sum_{t \le n} e^{\beta_0 V_t}\, e^{-\Delta u_k(t)}}{\sum_{t \le n} e^{\beta_0 V_t}},
\]

where \(V_t\) is the bias in force when sample \(t\) was taken. At convergence \(\Delta F(k) = -\beta_0^{-1} \ln \bigl( Z(\beta_k) / Z(\beta_0) \bigr)\) with configurational partition functions \(Z\), in energy units as in the PLUMED =DELTAFS= file. Before the first update, \(\Delta F\) is initialized from =observation_steps= unbiased samples by the same expression with \(V_t = 0\), and that initialization counts as one sample in later updates. =rgflux= updates \(\Delta F\) with the incremental log-sum-exp recursion of PLUMED's =OPES_EXPANDED=, including the running constant \(\mathrm{rct} = \beta_0^{-1} \ln \langle e^{\beta_0 V} \rangle\), which matters only for restarts.

The bias depends on the coordinates only through \(U\). With \(\mathbf{F} = -\nabla U\),

\[
-\nabla \bigl(U + V(U)\bigr) = \Bigl(1 + \frac{dV}{dU}\Bigr) \mathbf{F}, \qquad 1 + \frac{dV}{dU} = \sum_{k} w_k \frac{\beta_k}{\beta_0}, \qquad w_k = \frac{e^{-\Delta u_k + \beta_0 \Delta F(k)}}{\sum_j e^{-\Delta u_j + \beta_0 \Delta F(j)}},
\]

so the biased force is the unbiased force scaled by the mixture average of \(\beta_k / \beta_0\), and no collective variable gradient is needed. The factor lies between \(\beta_N / \beta_0\) and \(\beta_1 / \beta_0\). PLUMED applies the same factor in its =ENERGY= action as \(1 - f_U\), where \(f_U = -dV/dU\) is the force on the energy value. =OpesMultithermalSurface= returns \(U + V(U)\) as the engine energy and \((1 + dV/dU)\mathbf{F}\) as the force, so the two are an exact energy and gradient pair.

A frame sampled with bias \(V_t\) reweights to any \(T \in [T_1, T_N]\) with

\[
\ln w_t(T) = \beta_0 V_t + (\beta_0 - \beta) U_t,
\]

which is the sum of the PLUMED =REWEIGHT_BIAS= and =REWEIGHT_TEMP_PRESS= log weights @@rst::cite:`tribelloPlumed2014,plumedConsortium2019`@@. =MultithermalReweighting::delta_f= applies the same weights to give \(\Delta F(T)\) at any temperature in the range. The reweighted mean \(\bar{A} = \sum_t w_t A_t\) is a ratio estimator. Its standard error is the batch-means error of \(n w_t (A_t - \bar{A})\), the linearization of that ratio, so the correlation between frames enters through =stats::batch_means=. The Kish effective sample size \((\sum_t w_t)^2 / \sum_t w_t^2\) @@rst::cite:`kishSurveySampling1965`@@ reports how many independent frames the weights retain at each target.

** Assumptions and engine wiring

- The bias acts on the configurational energy at fixed volume. The engine thermostat must be canonical at \(T_0\): =run_opes_multithermal= returns =FluxError::InvalidConfig= when the thermostat \(k_{\mathrm{B}}T\) differs from the bias \(k_{\mathrm{B}}T_0\), and for white noise with \(\gamma = 0\).
- =run_opes_multithermal= takes any =E: DynamicsEngine + ?Sized= whose forces come from an =OpesMultithermalSurface= sharing the same =SharedOpesMultithermal=, such as =NativeEngine= or an engine over =RgpotSurface=. It checks every step that the shared surface evaluated the engine's current positions and returned the energy the engine reports, and returns =FluxError::InvalidConfig= otherwise. Engines that integrate forces outside =rgflux=, such as =LammpcEngine=, cannot take the bias.
- Step indexing follows PLUMED: the starting configuration is MD step =start_step=, the first call to =OpesMultithermal::update= after construction or restart is skipped unless =observation_steps= is 1, and the force at step \(n\) uses the estimates before that step's update. Updates act on absolute multiples of =pace=, so a restart restores the last step from the state and refuses a different =start_step=.
- A state holds \(\Delta F\) and =rct= at one \(T_0\) and \(k_{\mathrm{B}}\). =rgflux= records both and the reweighting range in the state and refuses to restart under different values. PLUMED states lack these entries, and PLUMED itself does not check them, so =rgflux= reads a PLUMED state only through =OpesMultithermal::from_plumed_state=, with the caller stating the \(T_0\) and \(k_{\mathrm{B}}\) it was written at.
- The weights are only as good as the overlap between the sampled energies and each target. Read the Kish effective sample size at each target before trusting an average there. The frames before initialization are unbiased samples at \(T_0\) and reweight correctly, but an early stretch with a rapidly changing bias is usually discarded by reweighting a slice.
- No =rgflux= module consumed reweighting weights before this one. =network::build_rate_table= is still unimplemented, so the per-frame weights are returned as plain =Vec<f64>= for downstream estimators.

* Kinetic rate networks (=network=)

=network= defines =NetworkConfig=, =RateTableConfig=, =RateEdge=, and =build_rate_table= for assembling elementary transition rates \(k_{i \to j}(T)\) @@rst::cite:`vineyardFrequencyFactors1957,henkelmanLongTimeScale2001`@@ into master-equation graphs and Bortz-Kalos-Lebowitz / Gillespie kinetic Monte Carlo (kMC) residence-time tables @@rst::cite:`goswamiEfficientExploration2025,goswamiConcurrencyAkmc2025,eon2901,bortzNewAlgorithmMonteCarlo1975,gillespieGeneralMethod1976`@@. =RateEdge::new= validates finite non-negative rate constants, while =build_rate_table= is not yet implemented and returns =FluxError::Unimplemented=.

* Copper vacancy mobility reference (=cu_reference=)

=cu_reference= packages the pure-Cu vacancy mobility column from Table II of Daw and Chandross (2021) @@rst::cite:`dawSluggishDiffusion2021`@@ (Physical Review Materials 5, 043603, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevMaterials.5.043603) strictly as a comparison denominator rather than an input to any rate computed by =rgflux=. In Eq. (5) of that paper, the mean-square displacement \(\operatorname{MSD}(t) = \langle\lvert\Delta \mathbf{u}(t)\rvert^2\rangle\) has long-time slope \(6\tilde{M}\) and total vacancy mobility \(M = N_{\mathrm{at}}\tilde{M}\). On an FCC lattice with nearest-neighbour coordination \(z = 12\) and jump distance \(a / \sqrt{2}\), a total vacancy hop rate \(\Gamma\) converts to mobility by \(M = \Gamma a^2 / z\) using the lattice constant \(a(T)\) measured at the simulation temperature.

Table II (p. 043603-8) reports the pure-Cu Arrhenius parameters \(M(T) = M_0 \exp(-Q_m / k_{\mathrm{B}}T)\) (Eq. 3) with \(M_0 = 190\,\text{Å}^2/\text{ps}\) and \(Q_m = 0.67\,\text{eV}\) (matching the FBD static saddle-point energy), together with pure-element statistical sampling errors of \(13\%\) (\(0.13\)) on \(\ln M_0\) and \(0.013\,\text{eV}\) on \(Q_m\). =cu_reference::run_npt_lattice= and =cu_reference::cu_vacancy_comparison= (exercised by =examples/cu_vacancy_adaptive.rs=) refuse explicitly until =rgpot-core= exports =eam_table_from_file= and =eam_table_from_data=, rather than substituting a stored lattice.
